# Research sources (Mode B)

A guided pass that produces raw inputs when the user has none. Budget about
45 minutes, monthly. The user does anything that needs a login; Claude can read
public pages (e.g. search suggestions, Google Trends) and helps log and cluster
findings. Use the market and language from the kit's `brand-profile.md` for all
searches.

Evidence note: the method comes from Sprout Social (social listening), Buffer
(reading comments), Content Marketing Institute and HubSpot (customer research),
and Google Trends guidance. Several are tool vendors; treat the advice as
directional. **Social and search findings are signals to validate, not proof of
what most customers think** (public audiences often differ from customers).

## 1. Comments on the brand's own Page and on adjacent Pages (free)

- Own Page: comments, DMs, questions on posts and ads.
- Adjacent Pages the audience follows (communities, associations, tool/hobby
  Pages, explainers in the brand's field): read the comments for recurring
  questions. Not competitors to copy.
- Log: the question as asked, the topic, how many people asked.

## 2. Search demand (in the brand's language and region)

- **Autocomplete and "People also ask"** for seed phrases built from the
  audience's problems in `brand-profile.md` (their words, "how to…", "why does
  my…", "X vs Y", "alternatives to X").
- **Google Trends** (set the region and language): compare 2–4 topics to see
  seasonality and rising interest (relative, not volume). Use it for timing.
- "X vs Y" and "alternatives" queries show what people are deciding between.
- Log: the query, the question form, seasonality note.

## 3. Reviews and complaints about alternatives

Public reviews/complaints about tools, agencies or competitors the audience
uses: what went wrong, what they wished for. Turn recurring complaints into
"how to avoid X" or "what to ask before choosing" posts. Log patterns, not names.

## 4. Competitor and adjacent post formats (topics and formats only)

Which *topics* and *formats* get comments on public posts in the niche.
Meta's Ad Library is public (search by Page name) and shows which offers a
competitor keeps running; its official API only covers certain ad types, so
don't expect full data. Don't use third-party ad-spy or scraping tools (terms,
privacy and reliability are unverified). Never copy text, structure-by-structure,
or creative. Record: topic, format, why it seems to work — or send the best
ones through Mode C (`inspiration-analysis.md`).

## 5. Real conversations (best source; see `interview-guide.md`)

2–3 short conversations with real customers beat any tool.

## From findings to ideas

1. Cluster findings by topic; a topic seen in 2+ sources outranks a single hit.
2. Write each cluster as one or more post-sized ideas (question answered,
   mistake + fix, myth, checklist step), phrased in the audience's words.
3. Add provenance (source + where) and format candidates in `idea-backlog.md`.
4. Mark social/search-derived ideas `needs validation` until confirmed.
